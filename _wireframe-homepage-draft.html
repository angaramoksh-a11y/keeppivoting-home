② The moment

Every business gets one moment where the ground moves.

It never announces itself. There is no memo, no bad quarter, no obvious villain. The phone rings the way it always has. The same customers find you the same way they always did. Everything feels permanent, right up until it isn't.

Underneath all of that, the ground starts to move. The way people find you changes. Quietly, while your numbers still look fine, the path that brought you every customer you have is being replaced by a different one.

Some businesses feel it coming and turn early. Some feel it and freeze. And a few never feel it at all, until the customers simply stop arriving and nobody inside can say when it started.

That one moment decides everything. Years of being good count for nothing if you stand still while the ground moves out from under you.

③ It has happened before

We already know how this story ends. We've watched it four times.

Kodak built the first digital camera. Their own engineer, in 1975. The prototype worked, the executives saw it, and they buried it, because every digital photo was a roll of film they would never sell. They understood the threat perfectly. They just chose to protect the old thing instead of becoming the new one. Kodak filed for bankruptcy in 2012.

Blockbuster had thousands of stores and the chance to buy a small, struggling DVD-by-mail startup called Netflix for fifty million dollars. The room laughed it off. You already know which of those two names is still around.

Netflix was that startup. Red envelopes in the mail, and it was working. Then they watched streaming coming and did the hardest thing a company can do: they walked away from the business that was paying the bills, before it forced them to. That is the only reason you have heard of them.

Apple was ninety days from bankruptcy in 1997. Not struggling. Ninety days from gone. Steve Jobs came back, cut most of the products, and aimed what was left at a future nobody else could see yet. The iMac. Then the iPod. Then the iPhone. Same company, same people. One decision to move.

Line the four of them up and the lesson is almost insulting in how simple it is. Kodak had the better technology. Blockbuster had the bigger business. Being good was never what saved anyone. Kodak and Blockbuster were the best in the world at the thing the world was walking away from. Netflix and Apple moved when the ground moved. That is the whole difference between the companies you still use and the ones your kids will never hear of.
